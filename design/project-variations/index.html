V1 · Focus

The conversation is the project

The idea

A project opens straight into Messages, one centred column at full width, under a slim one-line bar. Nothing sits beside the chat.

What it hides

Tasks and Info move into one slide-over (the Tasks 3 button, and ⓘ with a red dot while something is overdue). Files, Memory and the rest of the tabs move into ···.

Getting back

B goes to the Dashboard, Projects goes to all projects, and the project name ▾ switches projects directly. On a phone the ← arrow goes back to Projects.

Trade-off

This is the calmest option and the closest to how the client actually talks. Deadline and tasks are a tap away instead of always in view, so the overdue dot has to carry the warning.

V2 · Rail

Every project one click away

The idea

A fixed left rail holds the app's pages and every project with its unread count, like Slack or Linear. The open project fills one card on the right, with four tabs.

What it hides

The Info column shrinks to one line under the name (client · days left · health), with the details under ⓘ. The Tasks column becomes a tab. The top bar is gone.

Getting back

Dashboard and All projects are always the first rows of the rail, and any other project is one click away. On a phone it depends on the layout below.

Trade-off

This is the fastest way to move between projects, and it suits someone with many clients. It gives up 250px of width and changes the app's frame from a top bar to a sidebar on every page.

The rail on a phone

Pick one with the switch above the frame. It shows the phone width.

A · Drawer

The rail slides in from ☰, and the project keeps the whole screen. But the other projects and their unread counts stay hidden until you open it, and ☰ is easy to miss.

B · Stack, like Slack Chosen

The rail becomes the Projects screen: a Jump to a project search, then every project with its latest message, unread ones in bold with a count. The app's pages sit in a bottom bar on this screen only. A project opens full screen with no bottom bar, and ‹ 3 slides back, showing how many messages wait elsewhere. Coming back marks the project read.

C · Bottom bar

The rail's pages move to a bottom bar, and tapping the project name ▾ opens the project list as a sheet. The way back is always in view, but there are two rows of tabs (project tabs on top, app tabs at the bottom) and less height for the chat.

V3 · Project home

Open on what needs you

The idea

A project opens on an Overview: what needs you, the latest messages with a reply box, and the next tasks. The full chat, tasks, files and memory are tabs underneath.

What it hides

The full chat stays behind Messages until you want it. The Info column becomes three chips (health, days left, progress). The project bar and its ··· list get much shorter.

Getting back

The app's own top bar stays, with Projects lit, so Dashboard and Projects never move. A Projects › breadcrumb sits above the name.

Trade-off

This is the clearest summary and the most consistent with the rest of the app. Chatting takes one more click (or the reply box), so it fits better if you check in on projects than if you live in them.

V4 · Split

One list, one detail

The idea

Everything in the project is one list, like Mail: what needs you, the conversation, tasks, memory and files. Pick an item to open it on the right. A task shows the message it came from.

What it hides

All the tabs and side columns. One filter (All, Tasks, Memory, Files) replaces them. Health, days left and progress fit in one line above the list.

Getting back

B goes to the Dashboard, the Projects crumb goes to all projects, and the name ▾ switches projects. On a phone you see the list first, the detail slides over it, and ← goes back.

Trade-off

This makes the link between a message and the record it created very clear, and it works well on a phone. The chat becomes one item among many, which may feel less direct for a chat-first product.
